What we run

Two programmes, delivered inside the school

Both are turnkey. We bring the facilitators, the materials, the assessment technology and the post-event report. The school brings the room and the invitation.

Awareness Day

3 hours · parents · workshop + panel + networking

A focused parent event built for high visibility and low logistical load. It runs in any school — including government schools, with no phone or internet dependency — and is usually the right first engagement with a new institution.

Whole-School Day

Full school day · students, parents and teachers · three parallel tracks

The full institutional programme. Three tracks run simultaneously from morning to close, so the school moves as one body instead of sending a handful of parents to an evening talk.

Students

Two age-banded sessions, run without phones — pressure, comparison, and what to do when it turns on you.

Parents

The workshop and panel, the parenting assessment, and a printed family digital-wellness kit to take home.

Teachers

A counsellor-led session with a printed toolkit — what to escalate, what to hold, and how to spot the quiet ones.

Every engagement closes with a written report to the school: attendance, what the assessment surfaced across the cohort, and where to put attention next term. Programmes are delivered in government, national and international schools — the government version is entirely paper-based, with the same facilitators and the same report.

The record

TikTok chose Welmnt for Family Academy

TikTok brought the second edition of its regional Family Academy to Egypt and named Welmnt as its mental-health and family-wellbeing partner. The International School of Choueifat in 6th of October hosted. The subject was cyberbullying and adolescent mental health — and the room was the people who actually hold the phone conversations at home.

The workshop

An interactive parent session — متصلون لا مُراقَبون, Connected, Not Controlled — led by Dr. Walaa Elgammal: what cyberbullying actually does to an adolescent, and what a parent can say on a Tuesday night instead of confiscating a phone.

The panel

Moderated by Welmnt's founder, with Raghdah Alazab (Head of Communications, TikTok MENA), Dr. Walaa Elgammal, and Leila El Meligy, psychologist at the host school — working the problem from the platform, clinical and school sides at once.

The instrument

Parents scanned a QR on arrival, answered in Egyptian Arabic, and left with a personal report on their own parenting style. A live screen aggregated the room's answers and gave the panel something real to argue about.

Cyberbullying has real, deep effects on teenagers' mental health. Hazem Abdelghany · Founder, Welmnt · on the Family Academy panel

The instrument

A parenting assessment that speaks Egyptian

Not a translated Western questionnaire. The items are written in Egyptian colloquial Arabic, about situations that happen in an Egyptian home, and scored across five constructs. A parent finishes in ten minutes and gets a personal report before leaving the hall.

Instant report

Strengths, flags, one habit to start this week, and a sentence the parent can actually say to their child tonight — generated per respondent, in Arabic.

The room screen

A live aggregate of the hall's answers, projected behind the panel. It turns an abstract lecture into a conversation about the people sitting in it.

Paper parity

Identical items, identical output, on paper — so a school with no device assumption gets the same programme, not a reduced one.

Our story

Founded in 2024, in Egyptian schools

Welmnt began with one conviction: that understanding and nurturing mental wellbeing belongs in a school's ordinary week, not in a referral after something has already broken. We came up through an incubator, raised grant funding, signed our first schools, and learned the difference between a product a school admires and a programme a school books.

What survived that learning is what we do now: show up in the building, in Arabic, with the parents in the room — and leave the school with something it can act on.

Ask and we shall answer

Questions schools actually ask

Who actually delivers the sessions?
Dr. Walaa Elgammal — a PhD in mental health and psychotherapy — leads the parent and teacher sessions, with Welmnt facilitators on the student tracks. You are told before you book exactly who will be standing in your hall.
Is this therapy?
No. Welmnt is education and protection, not clinical treatment. We do not diagnose, label or treat. Where a session surfaces something that needs a professional, we say so to the school's counsellor through the proper channel — we don't handle it ourselves.
What happens to student and parent data?
Assessment responses are used to generate the respondent's own report and to produce a cohort-level summary for the school. Schools receive patterns, not private reflections. We do not sell data, and we do not pass individual responses to any third party — including a sponsoring brand.
Will this work in a government school?
Yes, and it has been designed to. The government version assumes no phones and no internet: identical items and identical output on paper, same facilitators, same written report to the school.
Can a brand or platform sponsor a programme?
Yes — that is how the TikTok Family Academy editions ran. The sponsor brings the product layer and the reach, Welmnt brings the psychological and educational layer, and the school brings the trust. The content stays ours.
